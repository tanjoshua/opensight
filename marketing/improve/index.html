Where actions come from

Two sources. No generic advice.

Most “AI SEO” advice is the same checklist handed to everyone. Improve only tells you things it can point at.

Half comes from a deterministic audit of your own website — facts about the web that are true for a clinic and a software company alike. The other half comes from your monitored answers: the sources ChatGPT cited when it recommended somebody else, read page by page and compared against what your site actually says.

Source 01 · Site audit

Seventeen checks against your own site.

Reachability, crawler access, indexing directives, sitemaps, canonicals, titles, and the structured data that states who and where you are. A failed check that has a fix becomes an action; the rest stay on the checklist as evidence.

Source 02 · Monitored answers

The pages that won the answers you lost.

When a source is cited across answers that omit you, OpenSight reads it. If it is a directory you are missing from, that is a listing to get. If it is a competitor’s page making a claim your site never makes, that is a content gap.

What we will not claim

No score. No grade. No credit we didn’t earn.

Whether a change moved a recommendation is a question nobody can answer honestly from one week of data, so Improve does not answer it. It will confirm that an issue is no longer found. It will never tell you that its advice caused your visibility to rise.

An empty queue says what it means.

“No actions found in the latest evidence” — not “your site is healthy”.

Nothing is invented.
